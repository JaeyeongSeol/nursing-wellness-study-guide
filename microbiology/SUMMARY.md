## L1 — Introduction (Chapter 1)

**Microorganisms** are organisms too small to be seen with the unaided eye. Few are pathogenic. Roles named on the slide: food spoilage, decompose organic waste, incorporate nitrogen gas into organic compounds, generate oxygen by photosynthesis, make ethanol, acetone, vitamins, fermented foods, and industrial products (cellulose, insulin, antibiotics).

**Human microbiome (microbiota):** microbes that live stably on/in the human body. 30 trillion body cells, 40 trillion bacterial cells. Acquired before birth. May stay indefinitely, or only fleetingly (**transient microbiota**). Help maintain health, can prevent growth of pathogens, may train the immune system.

**Three domains (Carl Woese, 1978),** based on cellular organization: **Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya** (protists, fungi, plants, animals). Types also listed: bacteria, fungi, protozoans, algae, viruses.

**Infection** results from invasion of part of the body by a microorganism. Those microbes are called infectious, infectious agents, pathogens, or pathogenic microorganisms.

**This class** studies infections by bacteria, fungi, viruses, and protozoa, and also helminths and arthropods. Helminths and arthropods are not microbes. They are parasites and vectors.

**Subdivisions:** bacteriology, virology, mycology, parasitology (protozoans, worms, insects). **Quiz:** fungal infections are studied by mycologists.

**Spontaneous generation:** living organisms arise from non-living matter. **Biogenesis:** living organisms arise from pre-existing life.

## L2 — Observing microbes (Chapter 3)

**Units (Table 3.1 on the slide):** 1 μm = 10⁻⁶ m. 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m. So 1 μm = 1,000 nm.

- 10 μm = 1 × 10⁻⁵ m = 10,000 nm. The slide asks this and does not fill it in. The table is enough to calculate it.
- **Quiz:** 100 nm = 0.1 μm.

**Compound light microscope path:** illuminator → condenser → specimen → objective lenses → ocular lenses. Total magnification = objective × ocular. A 100× objective and a 10× ocular = **1,000×**.

**Resolution:** ability of lenses to distinguish two points. Best light-microscope resolution on the slide is **0.2 μm** (2,000×). Shorter wavelengths give greater resolution.

**Brightfield:** direct light, dark objects on a bright background, staining increases contrast. **Darkfield:** only reflected light reaches the eyepiece; edges on a dark background. **Fluorescence:** UV or short wavelengths; fluorochromes emit visible light. **Electron:** beam of electrons, greater resolution, for objects smaller than about 0.2 μm. **TEM:** 2-D internal structures, 10,000–100,000×, resolution 2.5 nm. **SEM:** 3-D surface, 1,000–10,000×, resolution 20 nm. **Quiz:** the electron microscope has the highest magnification and greatest resolution of the options given.

**Smear**, then **fixing** (flame): kills, attaches, preserves. **Quiz:** fixing is not done to give bacteria a negative charge.

**Stains** are salts with a colored ion (chromophore). At pH 7, bacterial cells are slightly negative and attract positive ions. **Basic dyes:** chromophore is a cation (+); crystal violet, methylene blue, safranin. **Acidic dyes:** chromophore is an anion (−); negative staining, colorless cells. **Quiz:** acidic dyes are negative ions and are **repelled** by most bacteria.

| Stain type | Use, in slide words |
| --- | --- |
| Simple | Size, shape, and arrangement |
| Differential | Gram stain, acid-fast stain |
| Special | Capsule (negative), endospore, flagella |

**Gram (figure on L2 slide 20):** crystal violet (60 sec) → iodine (60 sec) → 95% ethyl alcohol (5–10 sec) → safranin (45 sec). Gram-positive stay purple or blue. Gram-negative are decolorized and stain red or pink with safranin. **Quiz order** uses the same four reagents and calls the decolorizer alcohol-acetone: **4-2-3-1** (crystal violet, iodine, alcohol-acetone, safranin). If the alcohol-acetone step is omitted, Gram-negative cells stay **purple**.

**Acid-fast:** primary stain carbolfuchsin (red), decolorize with acid alcohol, counterstain methylene blue. Acid-fast stay red (mycolic acid). Non-acid-fast stain blue. Examples: *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, *Mycobacterium leprae*.

## L3 — Structure (Chapter 4)

**Three domains again (Woese, 1978),** this time based on rRNA genes. Bacteria: pathogenic and non-pathogenic prokaryotes. Archaea: extreme environments, unusual metabolism (extreme halophiles, hyperthermophiles). Eukarya: animals, plants, fungi.

**Prokaryote vs eukaryote (slide bullets + Table 4.2)**

| | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chromosome | One circular chromosome, not membrane bounded; no histones | Paired / multiple linear chromosomes in a membrane; histones |
| Organelles | No membrane-bounded organelles | Membrane-bounded organelles |
| Wall | Peptidoglycan (bacteria) | Polysaccharide when present (cellulose, chitin) |
| Division | Binary fission | Mitotic spindle / mitosis |
| Size | Slide text: 0.2–1.0 μm × 2–8 μm. Table: typically 0.2–2.0 μm diameter | Table: typically 10–100 μm diameter. Slide text uses the same 10–100 μm range |
| Flagella | Flagellin | Tubulin, 9 pairs + 2 array |
| Ribosomes | 70S | 80S (70S in organelles) |

**Quiz:** plasma membrane and ribosomes = both. Binary fission, flagellin, and circular chromosomes = prokaryotes only. Lysosomes = eukaryotes only.

**Shapes:** coccus, bacillus, spirillum, spirochetes. **Arrangements:** diplococci, streptococci, staphylococci; diplobacilli, streptobacilli. Vibrio = curved (*V. cholerae*). Spirillum = rigid corkscrew, external flagella. Spirochete = flexible helix, **axial filament**. **Monomorphic** = one shape. **Pleomorphic** = many shapes (*Mycoplasma pneumoniae*).

**Glycocalyx:** capsule (neat, tightly packed, prevents phagocytosis) or slime layer (unorganized and loose). Example: *S. pneumoniae*.

**Flagella:** chains of flagellin, protein hook, basal body. Clockwise or counterclockwise. Arrangements **named** on Figure 4.7: peritrichous; monotrichous and polar; lophotrichous and polar; amphitrichous and polar. The slide does not write a sentence definition of each name. **Runs and tumbles. Chemotaxis. Random walk.** **Quiz:** “run” and “tumble” = cell mobility in response to attractants or repellents. A tuft at one end was scored as **lophotrichous**.

**Fimbriae** allow attachment. **Pili** are used to transfer DNA from one cell to another. **Quiz** also says pili are for transfer of DNA and for cell motility. Motility is not in the slide sentence.

**Cell wall** gives shape and prevents osmotic lysis. Gram-positive: peptidoglycan (NAG-NAM cross-linked by polypeptides), teichoic acid, lipoteichoic acid. **Penicillin** blocks peptidoglycan cross-linking. **Lysozyme** breaks peptidoglycan. Gram-negative: outer membrane + thin peptidoglycan; protection from phagocytes, complement, and antibiotics; **LPS (Lipid A is an endotoxin)**; O polysaccharide (example *E. coli* O157:H7); **porins**. **Quiz:** the wall with Lipid A is Gram-negative; the wall with teichoic acid is Gram-positive and is the one sensitive to penicillin; both walls protect against osmotic lysis.

**Atypical walls:** *Mycoplasma* has no wall, pleomorphic, sterols in the membrane. Acid-fast walls are like Gram-positive plus **mycolic acid** (*Mycobacterium*, *Nocardia*).

**Endospores:** highly durable dehydrated dormant cells; withstand boiling about 20 hours. Sporulation = spore formation. Germination = return to a vegetative cell. One spore gives rise to one bacterium. *B. anthracis*, *C. tetani*, *C. botulinum*, *C. perfringens*, *C. difficile*.

Eukaryotic organelles are **named** (ER, Golgi, lysosomes, mitochondria, chloroplasts). The slide asks for their functions and does **not** answer.

## L4 — Metabolism (Chapter 5)

**Metabolism:** the sum of the chemical reactions in an organism. **Catabolism:** breakdown of larger molecules to provide energy and building blocks for anabolism. **Anabolism:** uses energy and building blocks to build large molecules. **Metabolic pathway:** a sequence of enzymatically catalyzed chemical reactions in a cell.

**Catalyst:** increases the rate of a chemical reaction without itself undergoing any permanent chemical change. Almost all enzymes are proteins. Enzymes lower **activation energy** (an energy barrier). Factors: temperature, pH, substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, inhibitors. “What are ribozymes?” is asked and **not answered**.

**ATP** is the currency of chemical energy. Other forms: NADH and FADH2. ATP links catabolism and anabolism.

**Two ways to phosphorylate ADP**

- **Substrate-level phosphorylation:** a high-energy phosphate on a molecule is used to make ATP. Glycolysis and the TCA (Krebs) cycle. **Quiz:** the 1,3-diphosphoglyceric acid → 3-phosphoglyceric acid reaction is substrate-level phosphorylation.
- **Oxidative phosphorylation:** electrons from NADH and FADH2 go through the electron transport chain, build a proton gradient, and that gradient drives ADP → ATP.

**Aerobic respiration** uses three pathways: glycolysis, Krebs cycle, electron transport chain. The slide says Krebs and the ETC require oxygen. Most energy is released during the ETC and oxidative phosphorylation.

| Step | What the slide says |
| --- | --- |
| Glycolysis | Incomplete oxidation. 1 glucose → 2 pyruvic acid, net 2 ATP, 2 NADH |
| Intermediate | Pyruvic acid → acetyl CoA + CO2 (+ NADH). 1 glucose → 2 pyruvate → 2 acetyl CoA |
| Krebs | Acetyl CoA completely oxidized to CO2. Some ATP. NADH |
| ETC | Oxidizes NADH and FADH2. Electrons passed to O2. Proton gradient drives ATP by chemiosmosis. Each NADH = 3 ATP. Each FADH2 = 2 ATP |

**38 ATP** from complete oxidation of one glucose: glycolysis 2 ATP + 2 NADH; intermediate 2 NADH; Krebs 2 ATP + 6 NADH + 2 FADH2. Substrate ATP = 4. NADH = 10 × 3 = 30. FADH2 = 2 × 2 = 4.

**Fermentation (scientific definition on the slide):** releases energy from oxidation of organic molecules that does not require oxygen and does not use the Krebs cycle or the ETC. Final electron acceptor is an organic molecule (example: pyruvic acid). Does not completely break down sugars.

- Alcoholic: ethanol + CO2
- Lactic acid
- Homolactic: lactic acid only
- Heterolactic: lactic acid and other compounds

**Quiz:** best definition among the options = partial oxidation of glucose with organic molecules as electron acceptors. Yeast make ethanol when oxygen is limiting; if oxygen is present, alcohol is not the product. Lactic-acid-only fermentation = homolactic. ATP is produced in fermentation (by glycolysis). The statement “ATP is produced during respiration but not during fermentation” was a **wrong** statement on the quiz.

**Quiz pathway sort (answer key)**

| Statement | Quiz key |
| --- | --- |
| ATP by oxidative phosphorylation | ETC only |
| ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation | Glycolysis and Krebs |
| Majority of the cell’s ATP | ETC only |
| Cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells | Glycolysis only |
| Cytoplasm of bacterial cells | Glycolysis, Krebs, and ETC |

## L5 — Growth (Chapter 6)

**Five I’s:** inoculate, incubate, isolate, inspect, identify.

**Identification:** morphology (cell wall, shape), differential staining (Gram, acid-fast), biochemical tests (enzymes).

**Chemical needs:** carbon, oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, phosphorus, trace elements. **Physical needs:** temperature, pH, osmotic pressure. Also radiation and atmospheric pressure.

- **Autotrophs:** CO2 as carbon, inorganic salts as nitrogen.
- **Heterotrophs:** organic carbon.
- Nitrogen is used to make amino acids and proteins. The source of N is asked and **not answered**.
- Sulfur: amino acids, thiamine, biotin. Source: sulfate or H2S.
- Phosphorus: DNA, RNA, ATP, membranes. Source: inorganic phosphate.

**Oxygen groups**

| Group | Slide wording | Tube (figure) | Examples on the slides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Obligate aerobe | Requires O2; respiration; SOD and catalase | Top | *Micrococcus*, *Corynebacterium*, *P. aeruginosa*, *B. pertussis*, *M. tuberculosis* |
| Facultative anaerobe | Grows with or without O2; prefers O2; respiration and fermentation; SOD and catalase | Throughout, more at the top | *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *S. aureus*, *S. cerevisiae* |
| Obligate anaerobe | No SOD or catalase; mostly fermentation | Bottom only | *C. tetani*, *C. botulinum*, *C. difficile*, *Bacteroides* |
| Aerotolerant anaerobe | Does not require O2; SOD, some have catalase | Even | *Lactobacillus*, *Streptococcus* |
| Microaerophile | Higher CO2 and lower O2 than air (~2–10% on the tube slide); lower SOD and catalase | Band below the surface | *N. gonorrhoeae*, *N. meningitidis* |

**Quiz:** grows best with oxygen and can also grow without it = **facultative anaerobe**. A bacterium that can grow with or without oxygen was keyed as producing energy by glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.

Toxic oxygen products named: superoxide radical, hydrogen peroxide, hydroxyl radical. Aerobes make protective enzymes.

**Cardinal temperatures:** minimum, optimum, maximum.

| Group | Min | Optimum | Max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Psychrophiles | −10°C | 15°C | 20°C |
| Psychrotrophs | 0°C | 25°C | 30°C |
| Mesophiles | 15°C | 20–40°C | 45°C |
| Thermophiles | 45°C | 55–70°C | 80°C |
| Hyperthermophiles | 65°C | 85–95°C | 105°C |

Psychrotrophs spoil refrigerated food. Mesophiles are the common cause of food spoilage and disease. Thermophiles and hyperthermophiles are not called public health problems on the slide.

**pH optima:** acidophiles 0–5.5; neutrophiles 5.5–8; alkalophiles 8.5–11.5. *H. pylori* lives in the stomach under the mucus. How it survives is asked and **not answered**.

**Osmotic pressure:** high salt or sugar causes plasmolysis. Halophiles grow optimally at > 0.2 M. Non-halophiles < 0.2 M NaCl. Extreme (obligate) halophiles require > 2 M (30% salt). “Facultative halophiles — explain?” is asked and **not answered**. Hypertonic, hypotonic, and isotonic are asked and **not defined**.

**Media words:** culture medium, sterile (no living microbes), inoculum, culture. Agar liquefies at 100°C and solidifies at about 40°C. It is generally not metabolized. Chemically defined = all components known (minimum media). Complex = some components unknown (nutrient agar, nutrient broth).

| Functional type | Slide example |
| --- | --- |
| Enriched | Blood agar, chocolate agar. *H. influenzae* on chocolate agar (fastidious) |
| Differential | Blood agar (hemolysis) |
| Selective | MacConkey encourages some bacteria and suppresses others |
| Both | MacConkey: selects Gram-negative, differentiates lactose fermenters. Mannitol salt agar: selects Gram-positive, differentiates mannitol fermenters |

**Growth** = increase in number of cells, not cell size. Binary fission. Alternatives named: budding, conidiospores, fragmentation. **Generation time** = time for a cell to divide or a population to double. Average 1–3 hours. *E. coli* = 20 min. 20 generations (7 hr): 1 cell becomes 1 million.

| Phase | Slide wording |
| --- | --- |
| Lag | Making new enzymes for a new medium. Figure: intense activity preparing for growth, no increase in population |
| Log | Exponential. Most sensitive to drugs and radiation. Figure: increase is from binary fission (bacteria) or mitosis (yeast) |
| Stationary | Nutrients limiting or wastes toxic. Death rate = division rate |
| Death | Death exceeds division. Figure: population decreases at a logarithmic rate |

## L6 — Control (Chapter 7)

| Term | Slide wording |
| --- | --- |
| Sterilization | Destruction or removal of **all** forms of microbial life |
| Disinfection | Chemicals kill or inhibit disease-causing microorganisms. Does **not** kill all. Usually toxic to human tissues |
| Sanitization | Only reduces the population to acceptable standards (utensils) |
| Antisepsis | Chemicals used to treat or prevent infection of **living tissues** |
| -cide | Kills. Biocide, germicide, virocide, fungicide |
| -static | Inhibits growth. Bacteriostatic |
| Sepsis | Bacterial contamination |
| Asepsis | Absence of significant contamination |
| Aseptic techniques | Methods that minimize contamination |

**Effectiveness depends on:** larger population takes longer; different cells differ in sensitivity; higher concentration has a greater effect; longer exposure kills more; high temperature increases killing; organic material (sewage, biofilms) decreases killing; spores are more resistant than vegetative cells.

**Resistance, most to least (figure):** prions → bacterial endospores → mycobacteria → protozoan cysts → vegetative protozoa → Gram-negative bacteria → fungi, including most fungal spores → naked viruses → Gram-positive bacteria → enveloped viruses.

Slide sentences that match the figure: Gram-negative more resistant than Gram-positive; *P. aeruginosa* is the most resistant Gram-negative; acid-fast more resistant than non-acid-fast; endospores are the most resistant bacterial forms; naked viruses more resistant than enveloped viruses; cysts more resistant than trophozoites. *Cryptosporidium* cysts resist chlorine. Why *M. tuberculosis* resists aqueous bactericides, and why naked viruses are more resistant, are asked and **not answered** on L6. Mycolic acid is on L2 and L3.

**Death-time terms**

- **TDP:** lowest temperature that kills all microbes in a liquid culture in 10 minutes.
- **TDT:** time required to kill all bacteria in a liquid culture at a given temperature.
- **DRT:** time to kill 90% of the population at a given temperature.

Slide 8 says a higher concentration has a greater effect. Slide 10 says “the higher the conc. the longer it takes.” The slides do not reconcile those two sentences.

**Moist heat** kills by coagulation of protein. Steam is more effective than boiling. Endospores resist boiling. **Dry heat** kills by oxidation and needs higher temperatures and longer times (oven 170°C, 2 hours; also flaming and incineration).

**Autoclave:** 121°C, 15 psi, 15 min. Used on *C. botulinum* spores in canning. Why pressure must return to normal before opening, and why that is unnecessary for solid objects, are asked and **not answered**.

**Pasteurization** reduces pathogens and does not change taste. Milk treatment is designed to kill *M. tuberculosis*. Also kills *Streptococcus*, *Brucella*, and coliforms.

| Method | Numbers on the slide |
| --- | --- |
| Low-temperature hold (LTH) | 62.8°C for 30 minutes |
| High-temperature short-time (HTST) | 72°C for 15 seconds |
| Ultra-high temperature (UHT) | 5 sec cycle: 74°C → 140°C → 74°C |

**Other physical methods:** filtration sterilizes heat-sensitive liquids (membrane filters 0.1 mm thick, pores from 0.2 μm). Refrigeration is bacteriostatic. *Listeria monocytogenes* grows in the refrigerator. Desiccation is bacteriostatic. Lyophilization preserves cultures. Molds and yeasts resist drying and high salt. UV is non-ionizing, does not penetrate, damages eyes and DNA. Ionizing radiation (gamma, X-rays, electron beams) causes mutations and death and is used on spices, some meats and vegetables, and to sterilize medical supplies.

**Chemical points that are easy to mix up**

- Phenol: Lister. Lysol. Hexachlorophene works on *S. aureus* and is neurotoxic in rats. Triclosan was banned in US soaps in 2017.
- Chlorhexidine: biguanide; dentist use; skin and mucous membranes; low toxicity.
- Iodine: tincture (iodine + alcohol), iodophors (Betadine). Chlorine’s active form in water is hypochlorous acid. Chlorine is corrosive, inactivated by organic matter, and forms by-products linked to bladder cancer.
- Alcohols (ethanol, isopropanol) denature proteins and damage membranes. **More active at 70% than at 100%.**
- Heavy metals denature proteins: silver nitrate, mercury, copper sulfate (algicide), zinc.
- Soaps and detergents clean and have little disinfectant value.
- Quats are microbiocidal and more effective against Gram-positive cells. They do not work on endospores or mycobacteria. *P. aeruginosa* (slide spelling: Pseudomonas auriginosa) can grow in quats. Clinic note: *C. difficile*.
- Ethylene oxide denatures proteins, kills all microbes and spores, high penetration, long exposure, hospital sterilant. Chlorine dioxide is used on buildings and anthrax endospores.
- Hydrogen peroxide is a common antiseptic. Ozone is used by the GVRD so chlorine can be used at a lower concentration.
- Food preservatives named: sulfur dioxide, sodium benzoate, sorbic acid, calcium propionate, sodium nitrate and sodium nitrite.
- The antimicrobials in this lecture are not for ingestion or injection to treat disease. Antibiotics are “discussed later.”
