## 1. How the exam has been asking questions

The attached quiz is multiple choice, matching, and ranking. Patterns that already appeared:

- Match a person to one contribution (Koch, Fleming, Ehrlich, Hooke, van Leeuwenhoek, Pasteur).
- One blank in a sentence taken from an experiment (swan-neck flask excludes airborne bacteria).
- A unit conversion (nm and μm).
- Which microscope has the highest magnification and resolution.
- Gram stain order, color if a step is skipped, and what a purple cluster is.
- Acidic dye charge, and which reagent is the acid-fast decolorizer.
- Prokaryote vs eukaryote sorting.
- Flagella arrangement from a picture, and “run” / “tumble.”
- Which wall has Lipid A, teichoic acid, or penicillin sensitivity.
- Name the ATP-making process in a drawn reaction.
- Fermentation vs respiration, oxygen groups, and which statements about ATP are true.

Items you missed on that quiz are collected at the end of this file.

## 3. High-priority points

These are ideas the slides repeat, print in red or yellow, put in a “you are responsible” line, or put on both a lecture and the quiz. Treat them as likely exam content.

### Said twice, or said and then quizzed

1. **Woese, 1978, three domains.** L1 bases them on prokaryote vs eukaryote. L3 bases them on rRNA and adds what each domain is. Likely exam content.
2. **Spontaneous generation vs biogenesis, and the flask results.** Redi, Needham, Spallanzani, and Pasteur are all on the slides. The quiz asked who is tied to germ theory, what the swan neck excluded, and what an experiment needs in order to disprove spontaneous generation. Likely exam content.
3. **The 12 names on L1-18.** The slide says you are responsible for their contributions. Jenner and Linnaeus are on that list, and these slides never say what they did. Do not fill that gap from memory for this exam unless another course file says it.
4. **Gram-positive vs Gram-negative** is an objective on L2, a color rule, a procedure figure, Table 4.1, the wall slides on L3, and several quiz items. Color, thickness, teichoic acid, outer membrane, Lipid A, penicillin, and lysozyme are the cluster to memorize. Likely exam content.
5. **Acid-fast / mycolic acid / *Mycobacterium*** appears on L2, again as an atypical wall on L3, and again as a resistant group on L6. Likely exam content.
6. **Fimbriae vs pili, and flagella vs axial filaments,** are objectives. The quiz asked both the pilus function and a flagella-arrangement picture. Likely exam content.
7. **Prokaryote vs eukaryote** is the first objective of L3 and a quiz sorting item. Chromosome, histones, organelles, wall chemistry, division, flagellin vs tubulin, and 70S vs 80S. Likely exam content.
8. **Endospores** are on L2 (special stain), L3 (definition and the species list), and L6 (most resistant bacterial form; boiling does not kill them; the autoclave is aimed at *C. botulinum*). Likely exam content.
9. **Catabolism vs anabolism, and ATP between them,** are learning objectives on L4. Likely exam content.
10. **Respiration vs fermentation** is a learning objective, drawn twice, and is most of the metabolism quiz. Oxygen, Krebs, ETC, organic final acceptor, 38 ATP, NADH = 3, FADH2 = 2. Likely exam content.
11. **Substrate-level vs oxidative phosphorylation.** The quiz showed the 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate-style reaction and the answer was substrate-level. Likely exam content.
12. **The five oxygen groups** are an objective-level topic, a tube figure, Table 6.1, and the last quiz question. Likely exam content.
13. **Sterilization vs disinfection vs sanitization vs antisepsis, and -cide vs -static,** are the first objectives of L6. Likely exam content.
14. **The resistance order** is both a sentence list and a figure. Prions and endospores at the resistant end. Enveloped viruses and Gram-positive bacteria at the sensitive end. Likely exam content.
