## How to use this guide

1. Read the chapter file with the matching slide deck open.
2. Memorize the **Key definitions** in the slide’s own words. The slide number is the student-copy slide.
3. Study **Risk factors** by topic. If the slides do not split modifiable and non-modifiable, the guide says so.
4. Treat **High-priority points** as the most likely exam content. These are ideas the slides repeat, or ideas that appear in both the slides and the textbook.
5. Do the **practice questions** before opening the answer key. There are 10 questions for each chapter.

## What these materials do not contain

Do not fill these in from other sources for this exam. They are named on a slide, but the student slides do not give the list or the answer:

- The full **MAiD eligibility criteria**, and the names of the **two permitted forms** of MAiD. The slides say a person must meet all criteria, and that a grievous and irremediable condition also requires all of its criteria. They do not list those criteria. A search of the textbook text did not find “medical assistance in dying.”
- A bullet list of **dementia risk factors** on the dementia slide. The heading is there. The textbook discusses modifiable factors linked to heart-health prevention (see Chapter 14 file).
- Item-by-item scoring for **KATZ**. The slide names the tool. The textbook pages checked do not use the name “Katz.” The book does list examples of ADLs and IADLs (Chapter 5–7 file).
- The six areas inside the **48/6** tool. The slide says it covers six functional areas for adults 70+, in the first 48 hours. It does not name the six areas, and the textbook text search did not find “48/6.”
- Answers to the Chapter 15 slide questions “What is modern ECT?”, “Is ECT painful?”, “Does ECT cause brain damage?”, and “Is ECT risky for the elderly?” The slide asks them and does not answer them.
- Definitions of **aging anxiety**, **age attribution**, and the **anti-aging movement**. The slides name them as effects of ageism and do not define them.
