## 3. High-priority points

Likely exam content. These are repeated on the slides or appear in both the slides and the textbook.

- **Aging is normal and includes both losses and gains** (Slide 6). The slide asks what would be considered a gain but does not answer it.
- **Four ages** must stay distinct: subjective (how the person feels), perceived (how others estimate), chronologic (time since birth), functional (health, well-being, socioeconomic factors, and ability to participate) (Slide 4). The textbook also says functional age fits wellness-focused care.
- **Ageism changes care.** It influences policy, insurance reimbursement, and treatment regimens. People often receive less aggressive treatment because common ailments are dismissed as a natural part of aging. Older adults and providers may attribute symptoms to aging instead of reversible, treatable causes (Slide 9). The textbook repeats the false attribution of pathologic symptoms to normal aging as a barrier to wellness care.
- **How a nursing student counteracts ageism** (Slide 11): reflect on your own practice and become aware of institutional bias; have positive interactions with older adults; increase your knowledge to address misconceptions.
- **Gerontology is not geriatrics** (Slide 13, and again on the Ch. 5–7 deck). Gerontology = study of aging, holistic. Geriatrics = medical problems of older people.
- **Why this specialty matters** (Slide 15): it is core knowledge for all of nursing; the older proportion of the population is increasing and care of older persons is the fastest growing employment segment in health care; most nurses care for older people; the public expects nurses to help people age well.
- **Cultural continuum in slide order** (Slide 17): awareness → sensitivity → recognition → competence, and cultural safety is the recipient’s experience.
- **Do not replace the person with the group** (Slide 24): “Although developing cultural competency is an important part of nursing, we must avoid stigmatization or bias. Cultural generalizations can be useful and accurate, but they cannot replace individualized assessment and care.”
- **Canadian diversity facts** (Slides 19 and 21): about 20% of Canadians self-identify as from a visible minority (2/3 immigrated; 1/3 born in Canada). Largest specific cultural groups of older adults listed: South Asian Canadians, Chinese Canadians, Southeast Asian Canadians, Canadian Indigenous peoples.
- **Residential schools** are a discussion prompt only (Slide 22). The slides do not list nursing actions. Do not invent a protocol.
